# Shangrila (Godot recreation)

A Godot 4 recreation of *Shangrila*, an unfinished Macintosh adventure game written in
Pascal in 1997 (the original source, mainly `Shangrila.p` and `ShangrilaUtils.p`, is not included in this repository).

Run it: open the folder in Godot 4.7 and press Play, or run `godot-4 --path .` from this folder.

## What was carried over from the original

- **Screens and flow:** About picture, then main menu (New / Resume / Quit), Inn, View
  Character, Weapon Smith and the Mines. The original's Create Character screen and painted
  city view are retired: characters are pre-made, and Lehan is now a town map (see below).
- **Character rules:** the classes, their starting kits and encumbrance. (The original's rolled
  attributes and 22 skills are replaced by simpler abilities, see below.)
- **Items:** the full item pool (31 items) with the original weights, defense values, slot rules
  (`DoesItFit`), food effects (`EatAndDrink`), armour class and to-hit calculations.
- **Inn:** a roster of 23 characters, a party of 6, plus Muster / Dismiss / Info / Kill / scroll.
- **View Character:** drag and drop between the body slots and the 18-slot backpack. Drop an item
  on Info to examine it, on Eat to eat it, on Trash to throw it away, or on another character's
  portrait to give it to them.
- **Shop:** the Lehan weapon smith stock (including the three duplicate longswords), plus the
  basket and money display. Prices are now in silver.
- **Mines:** the exact 66-square map from `InitData`, with movement (turn, step, strafe), the
  compass and map mode with a pulsating party marker. The view is now real 3D instead of the
  original's pre-drawn strips.
- **Art:** the original PICT files in `Other Stuff` were decoded to PNG: the portraits
  "The Man" and "The Enemy", the sword, sky, rock textures and coins. The stone wall,
  floor tiles, parchment and chain textures are generated by `tools/make_textures.py`.

## What had to be filled in

- **Races:** the original Dwarf, Elf, Half Elf, Halfling and Human are replaced by Humans,
  Thuugs (slow, turtle-like folk with a shell), Aeldroes (slim, half-transparent beings with a
  gift for magic) and Nisk (wiry rat-folk). Old saves are converted when loaded.
- **Abilities and silver (new rules):** ten abilities from 0 to 10 (Combat, Missile Weapons,
  Toughness, Stealth, Thievery, Perception, Survival, Lore, Magic, Talk), shown as pips on the
  character sheet. Checks are d10 + ability against 6 / 9 / 12. The class sets the starting
  values and the *favoured* abilities (gold names, cheaper to raise), and the race adds +1 and a
  quirk. Hit points come from race + 3 × Toughness, mana from Magic. Experience is spent with
  the **+** buttons on the character sheet (the inn's *Info*): raising to N costs N × 100, or
  N × 150 if not favoured, and Humans pay 10% less. All money is silver. The rules are in
  `scripts/game.gd`; the full plan is "Simpler mechanics" in `IDEAS.md`.
- The resource forks (all UI pictures, the item icons and the 47 portraits) were empty in the
  copy, so the UI is rebuilt in code. Item icons and most portraits are drawn procedurally.
- Save and Resume were stubs in the original. Here they save to `user://shangrila_save.json`.
- Pay, Talk and Haggle in the shop had buttons but no logic. Pay now buys the basket, while Talk
  and Haggle only produce flavour text.
- Combat was never written (only the nine buttons existed), so it is new. See below.
  Examine only prints a message. Sleep now heals: it turns endurance (the apple bar) into
  hit points and restores mana, but monsters may interrupt.
- Six passages in the map lead to squares that were never defined (the map was unfinished).
  They show as blocked by rubble.

## Prologue (new)

*New* on the main menu starts with the opening scene from `DIALOGUES.md` in the repository root:
the spirit's eyes open on the Harrow Company's camp at dawn, it listens to their morning chatter
(its own thoughts in italics), chooses whom to hold on to, and makes the first choice through
that host in the new dialogue screen (keys 1-9). The scene moves on by itself; click or Space
hurries it, P pauses, and the mouse wheel (or H) opens the history of everything said so far. The screen shows the speaker, the host's inner voice in italics, a "Speaking" row
(only the host can speak for now) and replies tagged with the host's traits. The text is in
`scripts/data/prologue.gd`, the portraits for the four races in `scripts/widgets/race_portrait.gd`.

After the prologue the five members of the Harrow Company become the party, with the host first.
Their stats are rolled to fit their race and class, and their hidden traits are stored on them.
The party starts at the ruined shrine on the world map, a day from Lehan. The mines are not on
the map yet: the company takes the job at Lehan's council house, which the crier's notice in the
market square points them to.

## The road to Lehan and the first fight (new)

After the prologue the world map opens and the company sets out at once. The red marker moves
along the road while a dialogue window over the map plays the chatter (the companions begin to
notice something odd about the host, and the player chooses the host's reply). Then brigands
ambush the company: the first fight, in a battle window over the map. Everyone fights on their
own, by their tactics and traits (a Cowardly character hides, a Cautious one may parry). Before
each round the spirit can only help its host: *Guide the blow* (better aim and a little more
damage) or *Ward* (the next blow aimed at the host turns aside). Afterwards the companions
react to what they saw, and the spirit speaks to its host for the first time, asking to be
let in. The player picks the pitch: one or two of the four suit the host's traits (shown as a
hint), a wrong one gets pushback and another try, and taking hold by force always works but
leaves the host afraid. Then the company reaches Lehan.
The text is in `scripts/data/road.gd`, the window in `scripts/widgets/battle_window.gd`, and the
dialogue window used by both scenes in `scripts/widgets/dialogue_box.gd`.

## Arriving in Lehan (new)

The first time the company enters Lehan after the road, they walk through the 3D streets on
their own, from the gate down Tanners' Row to the employer's house, talking as they go. The
employer asks for proof and pays, and the pay is shared out with a twist that depends on the
host. Then they walk on to the inn's door, and the company splits up: everyone except the host
goes to the inn's list, and *Muster* starts a short talk to win each one back. Soured
companions want it put right first. The employer's house stays on the map.
The text is in `scripts/data/lehan.gd`.

## Town map (new)

Lehan is a town plan in the style of the world map (generated by `tools/make_textures.py`).
It shows the places found so far: at first the South Gate, the Market Square and the Inn. The
market's crier points out the Council House and the Weapon Smith. Places are
listed in `TOWNS` in `scripts/game.gd`.

## The streets of Lehan (new)

Lehan is walked in first person, like the mines (same keys; Space talks to whoever is straight
ahead or goes through the door, and walking into someone or a door does the same). Entering
Lehan from the world map starts at the South Gate. The gate road leads up to the market square,
with Tanners' Row and the employer's house off to the west; a lane runs east to the inn and a
street winds north-west to the weapon smith. Leaving the inn or the shop puts you back outside
its door.

The square has timber-framed houses, stalls, a well, a cart and the townsfolk: the crier by the
well (whose notice about the missing miners puts the council house on the map), Lisbet, Skee
and Boddo (the hooks for the Lehan quests to come), Old Wenna and Siv the herb seller. A guard
stands by the gate. The stone council house fills the north side of the square.

**Map** (or M) shows the town plan with the places found so far; click one to go straight to
its entrance.

The 3D view (`scripts/widgets/dungeon_view.gd`) shows an *area* object instead of the mines
when one is set; the streets are `scripts/areas/lehan_streets.gd`, the screen
`scripts/screens/streets.gd`, the people and their lines `scripts/data/market.gd`, the
cut-out figures `scripts/widgets/figure_art.gd`, and the house textures come from
`tools/make_textures.py`.

## The Lehan quests (new; two of three built)

After the split the host is alone, and the crier has no news until the host has done one of the
Lehan quests for Lisbet, Skee or Boddo in the square. Each quest wins its character, who then
waits on the inn's list (a little better than the old companions, and harder to get). Only
Lisbet's is written so far:

**Lisbet's letter.** Carry a sealed letter down the gate road to Sergeant Rudd. Nobody may
follow, nobody may open it. On the gate road someone does follow: look (Perception), hide
(Stealth), call the sergeant (Talk), let the spirit listen (its one nudge on the quest), or walk
on. At the gate the letter can be opened (Thievery, or with the spirit's help) or handed over.
It is a test, and every host passes it their own way; Lisbet's fee for mustering is 20 silver,
or 40 if the host was followed, broke the seal or was caught lying. The letter tells what the
council really has for the miners (400 silver), which gives a strong argument at the council.
Checks are rolled when a scene starts; passing them and finishing the quest give story
experience. The quests are in `scripts/data/lehan_quests.gd` (shared: state, the new
characters, effects, mustering), `scripts/data/quest_lisbet.gd` and `scripts/data/quest_boddo.gd`.

**Boddo's debt.** Tam Reed, a porter who once pulled Boddo out of the river, died cheated of
forty silver by Master Gell, and Boddo swore Tam's widow would be paid. First the proof, in
Gell's Warehouse (a new door where the smith's street meets the square): talk your way to a look
at the wage book and spot the lie (Talk, then Perception or Lore), lift it (Thievery), come back
after dark (Stealth, with a scuffle with the watchman if it goes wrong), or let the spirit look.
Each way can be tried once, one per visit. Then Master Gell: shame him in front of his porters
(Talk) or lean on him (Combat; a scuffle if he calls his men). If both fail he burns the page.
At any point the host can pay the forty silver themselves. Boddo joins for no fee. The checks use
the best in the party, so a full company has more ways through than a lone host; a lost scuffle
costs everyone a few hits.

**Errands.** Once the mines job is cried, the first companion in the party grumbles when a quest
starts (each in their own voice), and Old Wenna wonders why you're still in town. This stops
after a trip into the mines.

## The council house (new)

The crier's notice opens the council house on the north side of the square, and the old
companions at the inn will listen from then on, so the player can choose who to bring. Three
councillors sit behind a table: Alderman Brannoc (who distrusts outsiders), Mistress Brask (who
keeps the purse) and Master Hale (whose family owns the old workings). They offer pay for news
of the six missing miners, and the company can haggle:
- **who comes matters:** a lone host gets a lower offer; Joss's name is known and raises it; a
  Nisk costs the council's patience; a Thuug and an Aeldroe draw remarks;
- **arguments:** the danger (Talk) and pressing Hale about what is up there (Perception), each
  spoken by the best in the party, and one from the host's own traits (a bold demand for
  double, a plea for the miners' families, half paid up front, asking what the miners found...);
- **patience:** a failed argument wears the council's patience; when it runs out they make a
  final offer. "We'll think on it" leaves and keeps the offer for another visit.

Taking the job puts the Mines on the world map. Reporting back after the mines is not built yet.
The text and rules are in `scripts/data/council.gd`, the screen in `scripts/screens/council.gd`.

## World map (new)

The town gate opens a map of the known lands (generated by `tools/make_textures.py`). It shows
the places the party has discovered, with dashed routes between them, and a red ring where the
party is. Click the party's own place (or *Enter*) to go in, or another place to travel there (the red
marker moves along the road).
Each day on the road costs every character 8 Endurance (the apple bar), so bring food. There are
three places so far: the Old Shrine, Lehan and the Mines, each a day apart along the road. Places and routes are listed in
`LOCATIONS` and `ROUTES` in `scripts/game.gd`.

## Combat (new)

Monsters can appear as you walk (12% per step after a few quiet steps). The deeper you go, the
tougher they get: rats, bats and kobolds near the entrance, then goblins, spiders and skeletons,
and finally orcs and renegades (the surviving "The Enemy" painting).

- **Turn based:** each round you give every living character an order (Attack, Parry, Spells,
  Use, Equip, Turn, Flee, Hide, Pass), clicking the monster group or portrait to target. Then
  everyone acts in initiative order and the log plays out at a readable pace. Click the log or
  press Space to hurry. Esc takes back the previous order.
- **Default actions:** *Tactics* sets a default action per character. *Defaults* gives everyone
  who still has no order their default and fights the round.
- **Auto-combat** (off at the start of every fight): toggle *Auto* and every round uses the
  default actions and races through. Untoggle it to take over again.
- Rules: to hit, d10 + Combat (Missile Weapons for bows) must reach the monster's defence; a
  monster hits on d10 + its attack against 8 + Combat + half the armour. Weapons have damage
  ranges. The first three party members take most of the blows. Parry makes you harder to hit.
  Hide makes monsters ignore you and doubles your next hit. Clerics and paladins can turn undead.
  Bows need arrows somewhere on the character (they are picked up again after the fight).
- Spells: mages (Fire Dart), enchanters (Slumber) and clerics (Mend) with at least 1 in Magic
  get a mana pool of 2 + 3 × Magic (the original kept max magic at 0).
- Winning gives experience, silver and sometimes an item. Experience is scaled so grinding does
  not pay: a fight at a character's own power (best fighting ability + Toughness) gives 1% of
  their next raise, each monster level above doubles it, each below halves it, and three below
  gives nothing. Hiding the whole fight gives nothing, falling halves it. Most experience comes
  from the story. Fallen characters come around with 1 hit point. If the whole party falls,
  they wake up at the inn, robbed of half their silver.

## Controls in the mines

W/↑ forward, S/↓ back, A/← and D/→ turn, Q/E step sideways, M map.
In combat: A Attack, P Parry, S Spells, U Use, E Equip, T Turn, F Flee, H Hide, X Pass,
D Defaults, Esc back, Space hurry.

## Developer aid

**Checkpoints** for testing by hand: *Start at...* on the title screen, or
`godot-4 --path . -- --start=market --host=tikkat`. The checkpoints are `road` (just after the
prologue), `lehan` (at the gate, before the employer), `split` (the inn after the split),
`market` (the market square, before the crier), `council` (after the crier, at the council
house's door, the company still at the inn) and `mines` (job taken, company back together). The
host is any of joss, maera, ommuk, ithrae, tikkat. The story before the checkpoint is filled in
with simple choices (see `scripts/checkpoints.gd`).


`godot-4 --path . -- --tour=$PWD/.tour` visits every screen, saves screenshots into `.tour/`,
and runs a few rule checks (see `scripts/dev_tour.gd`). It also simulates 200 auto-fights
at several depths and prints the win rate, which is handy when tuning monsters in `scripts/combat.gd`.
