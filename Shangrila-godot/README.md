# Shangrila (Godot recreation)

A Godot 4 recreation of *Shangrila*, an unfinished Macintosh adventure game written in
Pascal in 1997 (the original source, mainly `Shangrila.p` and `ShangrilaUtils.p`, is not included in this repository).

Run it: open the folder in Godot 4.7 and press Play, or run `godot-4 --path .` from this folder.

## What was carried over from the original

- **Screens and flow:** About picture, then main menu (New / Load / Quit), Inn, View
  Character, Weapon Smith and the Mines. The original's Create Character screen and painted
  city view are retired: characters are pre-made, and Lehan is now a town map (see below).
- **Character rules:** the classes, their starting kits and encumbrance. (The original's rolled
  attributes and 22 skills are replaced by simpler abilities, see below.)
- **Items:** the full item pool (31 items) with the original weights, defense values, slot rules
  (`DoesItFit`), food effects (`EatAndDrink`), armour class and to-hit calculations.
- **Inn:** a roster of 23 characters, a party of 6, plus Muster / Dismiss / Info / Kill / scroll.
- **View Character:** drag and drop between the body slots and the 18-slot backpack. Drop an item
  on Info to examine it, on Eat to eat it, on Trash to throw it away, or on another character's
  portrait to give it to them. The same items **stack** in a backpack slot, with the count in the
  lower right corner (quest items never stack, and worn or held slots hold one). Dragging moves
  the whole stack, except onto a body slot (one) or Eat (one); stacks merge when dropped on each
  other, and older saves have their loose items gathered into stacks.
- **Shop:** the Lehan weapon smith stock (including the three duplicate longswords), plus the
  basket and money display. Prices are now in silver. *Sell* shows what the smith buys from the
  packs (weapons, bows, arrows, quivers, armour and shields, the ore): half the price, the ore
  all of it; click sells one, Shift-click the lot. *Haggle* (once a day) has the best talker roll
  Talk: 10% better prices both ways against Normal, 20% against Hard. *Talk* gives news and hints
  that fit the story: the missing miners, then advice for the mines, then the marsh-folk.
- **Mines:** movement (turn, step, strafe), the compass and map mode with a pulsating party
  marker. The view is now real 3D instead of the original's pre-drawn strips. The original's
  66-square map was replaced by three new levels (see "The mines" below).
- **Art:** the original PICT files in `Other Stuff` were decoded to PNG: the portraits
  "The Man" and "The Enemy", the sword, sky, rock textures and coins. The stone wall,
  floor tiles, parchment and chain textures are generated by `tools/make_textures.py`.

## What had to be filled in

- **Races:** the original Dwarf, Elf, Half Elf, Halfling and Human are replaced by Humans,
  Thuugs (slow, turtle-like folk with a shell), Aeldroes (slim, half-transparent beings with a
  gift for magic) and Nisk (wiry rat-folk). Old saves are converted when loaded.
- **Abilities and silver (new rules):** ten abilities from 0 to 10 (Combat, Missile Weapons,
  Toughness, Stealth, Thievery, Perception, Survival, Lore, Magic, Talk), shown as pips on the
  character sheet. Checks are d10 + ability against 6 / 9 / 12. The class sets the starting
  values and the *favoured* abilities (gold names, cheaper to raise), and the race adds +1 and a
  quirk. Hit points come from race + 3 × Toughness, mana from Magic. Experience is spent with
  the **+** buttons on the character sheet (the inn's *Info*): raising to N costs N × 100, or
  N × 150 if not favoured, and Humans pay 10% less. The sheet shows the experience earned in all and
  what is unspent; a gold star on a portrait means that character can afford a raise. All money is silver. The rules are in
  `scripts/game.gd`; the full plan is "Simpler mechanics" in `IDEAS.md`.
- The resource forks (all UI pictures, the item icons and the 47 portraits) were empty in the
  copy, so the UI is rebuilt in code. Item icons and most portraits are drawn procedurally.
- Save and Resume were stubs in the original. Here *Save* picks one of three slots
  (`user://save_1.json` .. `save_3.json`), and *Load* on the title screen lists them with the
  automatic checkpoint save (`save_auto.json`): each shows its place, day and party (♥ fine,
  ✚ badly wounded, † dead). An old `shangrila_save.json` becomes slot 1.
- Pay, Talk and Haggle in the shop had buttons but no logic. Now all three work (see Shop above).
- Combat was never written (only the nine buttons existed), so it is new. See below.
  Examine only prints a message. Sleep now heals: it turns endurance (the apple bar) into
  hit points and restores mana, but monsters may interrupt.
- Six passages in the map lead to squares that were never defined (the map was unfinished).
  They show as blocked by rubble.

## Prologue (new)

*New* on the main menu starts with the opening scene from `DIALOGUES.md` in the repository root:
the spirit's eyes open on the Harrow Company's camp at dawn, it listens to their morning chatter
(its own thoughts in italics), chooses whom to hold on to, and makes the first choice through
that host in the new dialogue screen (keys 1-9). The scene moves on by itself; click or Space
hurries it, P pauses, and the mouse wheel (or H) opens the history of everything said so far. The screen shows the speaker, the host's inner voice in italics, a "Speaking" row
(only the host can speak for now) and replies tagged with the host's traits. The text is in
`scripts/data/prologue.gd`, the portraits for the four races in `scripts/widgets/race_portrait.gd`.

After the prologue the five members of the Harrow Company become the party, with the host first.
Their stats are rolled to fit their race and class, and their hidden traits are stored on them.
The party starts at the ruined shrine on the world map, a day from Lehan. The mines are not on
the map yet: the company takes the job at Lehan's council house, which the crier's notice in the
market square points them to.

## The road to Lehan and the first fight (new)

After the prologue the world map opens and the company sets out at once. The red marker moves
along the road while a dialogue window over the map plays the chatter (the companions begin to
notice something odd about the host, and the player chooses the host's reply). Then brigands
ambush the company: the first fight, in a battle window over the map. Everyone fights on their
own, by their tactics and traits (a Cowardly character hides, a Cautious one may parry). Before
each round the spirit can only help its host: *Guide the blow* (better aim and a little more
damage) or *Ward* (the next blow aimed at the host turns aside). Afterwards the companions
react to what they saw, and the spirit speaks to its host for the first time, asking to be
let in. The player picks the pitch: one or two of the four suit the host's traits (shown as a
hint), a wrong one gets pushback and another try, and taking hold by force always works but
leaves the host afraid. Then the company reaches Lehan.
The text is in `scripts/data/road.gd`, the window in `scripts/widgets/battle_window.gd`, and the
dialogue window used by both scenes in `scripts/widgets/dialogue_box.gd`.

## Arriving in Lehan (new)

The first time the company enters Lehan after the road, they walk through the 3D streets on
their own, from the gate down Tanners' Row to the employer's house, talking as they go. The
employer asks for proof and pays, and the pay is shared out with a twist that depends on the
host. Then they walk on to the inn's door, and the company splits up: everyone except the host
goes to the inn's list, and *Muster* starts a short talk to win each one back. Soured
companions want it put right first. The employer's house stays on the map.
The text is in `scripts/data/lehan.gd`.

## Town map (new)

Lehan is a town plan in the style of the world map (generated by `tools/make_textures.py`).
It shows the places found so far: at first the South Gate, the Market Square and the Inn. The
market's crier points out the Council House and the Weapon Smith. Places are
listed in `TOWNS` in `scripts/game.gd`.

## The streets of Lehan (new)

Lehan is walked in first person, like the mines (same keys; Space talks to whoever is straight
ahead or goes through the door, and walking into someone or a door does the same). Entering
Lehan from the world map starts at the South Gate. The gate road leads up to the market square,
with Tanners' Row and the employer's house off to the west; a lane runs east to the inn and a
street winds north-west to the weapon smith. Leaving the inn or the shop puts you back outside
its door.

The square has timber-framed houses, stalls, a well, a cart and the townsfolk: the crier by the
well (whose notice about the missing miners puts the council house on the map), Lisbet, Skee
and Boddo (the hooks for the Lehan quests to come), Old Wenna and Siv the herb seller. A guard
stands by the gate. The stone council house fills the north side of the square.

**Map** (or M) shows the town plan with the places found so far; click one to go straight to
its entrance.

The 3D view (`scripts/widgets/dungeon_view.gd`) shows an *area* object instead of the mines
when one is set; the streets are `scripts/areas/lehan_streets.gd`, the screen
`scripts/screens/streets.gd`, the people and their lines `scripts/data/market.gd`, the
cut-out figures `scripts/widgets/figure_art.gd`, and the house textures come from
`tools/make_textures.py`.

## The Lehan quests (new)

After the split the host is alone, and the crier has no news until the host has done one of the
Lehan quests for Lisbet, Skee or Boddo in the square. Each quest wins its character, who then
waits on the inn's list (a little better than the old companions, and harder to get). Only
Lisbet's is written so far:

**Lisbet's letter.** Carry a sealed letter down the gate road to Sergeant Rudd. Nobody may
follow, nobody may open it. On the gate road someone does follow: look (Perception), hide
(Stealth), call the sergeant (Talk), let the spirit listen (its one nudge on the quest), or walk
on. At the gate the letter can be opened (Thievery, or with the spirit's help) or handed over.
It is a test, and every host passes it their own way; Lisbet's fee for mustering is 20 silver,
or 40 if the host was followed, broke the seal or was caught lying. The letter tells what the
council really has for the miners (400 silver), which gives a strong argument at the council.
Checks are rolled when a scene starts; passing them and finishing the quest give story
experience. The quests are in `scripts/data/lehan_quests.gd` (shared: state, the new
characters, effects, mustering), `scripts/data/quest_lisbet.gd` and `scripts/data/quest_boddo.gd`.

**Boddo's debt.** Tam Reed, a porter who once pulled Boddo out of the river, died cheated of
forty silver by Master Gell, and Boddo swore Tam's widow would be paid. First the proof, in
Gell's Warehouse (a new door where the smith's street meets the square): talk your way to a look
at the wage book and spot the lie (Talk, then Perception or Lore), lift it (Thievery), come back
after dark (Stealth, with a scuffle with the watchman if it goes wrong), or let the spirit look.
Each way can be tried once, one per visit. Then Master Gell: shame him in front of his porters
(Talk) or lean on him (Combat; a scuffle if he calls his men). If both fail he burns the page.
At any point the host can pay the forty silver themselves. Boddo joins for no fee. The checks use
the best in the party, so a full company has more ways through than a lone host; a lost scuffle
costs everyone a few hits.

**Skee's lights.** Skee saw blue lights in the old tunnels under the town and ran, dropping his
bag. The way down is under the tannery on Tanners' Row (a new door): pick the grate (Thievery),
talk the tanner round (Talk), find the loose drain (Perception), let the spirit feel for cold air,
or pay her 5 silver. Below is a small 3D maze lit by your torch (`scripts/areas/lehan_tunnels.gd`,
screen `scripts/screens/tunnels.gd`): a rat nest blocking the way east (Stealth, Survival or
Combat; failing drives you back, bitten), a collapse (Perception or Survival, or Toughness; failing
costs hits), Skee's bag in a side passage, and the chamber of the lights: behind an old bricked-up doorway,
the shattered remains of a figure, broken on purpose, with part of what might be a crown and a few
shards still glowing. Reading what's left of the carvings (Lore) or holding a shard (Magic) tells
more, and the spirit can reach for the shards and almost remember something. Each failed try at the rats or the
collapse gives +1 on the next. Walk into the ladder to climb out. Then Skee must be persuaded it
wasn't ghosts, one argument per talk (the glowing shard, what the carvings said, his bag, the host's
calm, or simply company); if every argument fails, he comes anyway. He joins for no fee. The text
is in `scripts/data/quest_skee.gd`.

**Errands.** Once the mines job is cried, the first companion in the party grumbles when a quest
starts (each in their own voice), and Old Wenna wonders why you're still in town. This stops
after a trip into the mines.

**Skill checks on replies.** A reply that rolls a check shows the ability as a tag in the reply
(`[Combat]`, `[Talk, then Perception or Lore]`), coloured by the chance of success: red, orange,
yellow, green, grey for a sure thing. Hovering shows who rolls, their skill, any bonus and where
it comes from, and the chance.

**Failing costs experience.** Every failed check in a quest's scenes takes 10% off the story
experience for finishing it, down to half. The quest's last scene says how much was left
("Story experience: 80%, after two failed tries."). The +1 for each failed try at the rats or the
collapse stays, so nobody gets stuck.

**The quest log.** The Quests button (or J) in the streets, the tunnels, the town map, the world
map, the mines and the character screen opens a window over the screen with the current, not yet
taken and finished quests. Each keeps every scene of the quest as it was played: narration,
speech, the inner voice, the replies chosen (▸, with the check and its chance) and whether each
check passed. Kept in the save (`scripts/data/journal.gd`, `scripts/widgets/quest_log.gd`).

**Quest items.** Lisbet's letter, Tam's page from the wage book, Skee's bag and the glowing shard
are real items in a pack, marked with a gold ★, as is the Crowned Head from the shrine. They
can't be thrown away (the shop doesn't buy anything yet), can be passed around the party, and go
to someone who stays when their carrier leaves the party (the split, or dismissing at the inn;
dismissing the last one who could take them is refused). They come and go with the quests
(`LehanQuests.sync_items`), so older saves get theirs on loading. Tam's page can be read with
Info.

## The council house (new)

The crier's notice opens the council house on the north side of the square, and the old
companions at the inn will listen from then on, so the player can choose who to bring. Three
councillors sit behind a table: Alderman Brannoc (who distrusts outsiders), Mistress Brask (who
keeps the purse) and Master Hale (whose family owns the old workings). They offer pay for news
of the six missing miners, and the company can haggle:
- **who comes matters:** a lone host gets a lower offer; Joss's name is known and raises it; a
  Nisk costs the council's patience; a Thuug and an Aeldroe draw remarks;
- **arguments:** the danger (Talk) and pressing Hale about what is up there (Perception), each
  spoken by the best in the party, and one from the host's own traits (a bold demand for
  double, a plea for the miners' families, half paid up front, asking what the miners found...);
- **patience:** a failed argument wears the council's patience; when it runs out they make a
  final offer. "We'll think on it" leaves and keeps the offer for another visit.

Taking the job puts the Mines on the world map. Afterwards the council asks for news: once the
survivors are out, the foreman's fate is known and Saelen is home, the company reports and is paid
(see "The mines"). The text and rules are in `scripts/data/council.gd`, the screen in
`scripts/screens/council.gd`.

## The mines (new)

Three levels of 16 × 16, drawn as text maps in `scripts/areas/mines.gd` (MinesArea), with their own
textures from `tools/make_textures.py`. What happens in them is in `scripts/data/mines_quest.gd`
(MinesQuest). The dungeon screen (`scripts/screens/dungeon.gd`) walks them; Space (or Examine)
uses what is straight ahead, and with nothing there searches the walls for anything hidden.
Chests, levers, lifts and ladders sit in niches off the passages and are used from in front, so
they never block the way. Clicking a portrait opens that character's sheet; Leave comes back.
The map (M) draws every square you have walked and marks what you have seen beside it (camps,
chests, levers, lifts, ways up and down, blocked ways, people), with a legend underneath that
lists only what has been found on that level.
Thin mineral threads (helictites) hang from the ceilings here and there, twisting and branching
like spider's legs; they are grown from each square's coordinates, so they never move.

1. **The old workings** (rough rock and timbering): rats, bats and kobolds; a kobold den and a
   rat nest; the miners' cold camp with a spare lamp; an ore cart jammed across the rails to the
   shaft (push it, free the brake, or throw the points lever elsewhere); a stash behind boards
   that Examine (Perception) finds; rotten boards over a pit; the broken lift by the entrance.
2. **The new diggings** (pale rock, ore veins): Gloomweavers, Cave Lurkers and Delvers; bad air
   (the lamp shows the way, or Survival, or hold your breath); the breach, with the foreman and
   Tobin dead and the foreman's key; the locked stores (key, Thievery or force) with the lift
   crank, a steel sword and cap; ore veins to chip with a pick (the smith buys the ore); three
   survivors cut off behind a Gloomweaver nest; beside them, a flooded passage cuts off the lower
   gallery with the breach and the way down. Its wheel and drain lever are on the near side: the
   drain only works if the upper gate is shut first (a note says so). The foreman's key lies at
   the breach, so before draining, the stores open only by Thievery or force.
3. **The sealed galleries** (the jailers' fitted stone): the Oathbound, Oath-Archers and a Sworn
   Knight; a ward door opened by touching three carved pillars in the order an inscription gives
   (Lore reads it; or trial and error, with a jolt for each mistake); the jailers' armoury with an
   old blade and shield (they bite deeper into the oath-bound, and the host dislikes them), the
   Holm seal and a jailer's record that puts the Drowned Shrine in the Thuug marshes on the world
   map; Saelen in a trance (Magic, Talk, the spirit, or carry her); and the Warden before the tomb
   gate, which no blow can harm. Fleeing from it always works: it doesn't follow.

Placed groups stay dead once beaten; wandering monsters keep coming, tougher on each level. Each
level has a camp where resting is safe. Fitting the crank makes the lift work between every level
reached. The way out is the entrance on the first level. Hal Venner, the foreman's son, waits in
the market square once the job is taken; after the report he joins the inn's list. Failed checks
in the mines cost quest experience like the Lehan quests.

## World map (new)

The town gate opens a map of the known lands (generated by `tools/make_textures.py`). It shows
the places the party has discovered, with dashed routes between them, and a red ring where the
party is. Click the party's own place (or *Enter*) to go in, or another place to travel there (the red
marker moves along the road).
Each day on the road costs every character 8 Endurance (the apple bar), so bring food. There are
three places so far: the Old Shrine, Lehan and the Mines, each a day apart along the road (and the
Drowned Shrine, three days off, once the jailer's record is found). Places and routes are listed in
`LOCATIONS` and `ROUTES` in `scripts/game.gd`.

## Combat (new)

Monsters can appear as you walk (12% per step after a few quiet steps). Each level of the mines
has its own: rats, bats and kobolds in the old workings, Gloomweavers, Cave Lurkers and Delvers
in the new diggings, and the jailers' oath-bound dead in the sealed galleries. Placed groups
(dens and guards) stand where they are until beaten.

- **Turn based:** each round you give every living character an order (Attack, Parry, Spells,
  Use, Equip, Turn, Flee, Hide, Pass), clicking the monster group or portrait to target. Then
  everyone acts in initiative order and the log plays out at a readable pace. Click the log or
  press Space to hurry. Esc takes back the previous order.
- **Default actions:** *Tactics* sets a default action per character. *Defaults* gives everyone
  who still has no order their default and fights the round.
- **Auto-combat** (off at the start of every fight): toggle *Auto* and every round uses the
  default actions and races through. Untoggle it to take over again.
- Rules: to hit, d10 + Combat (Missile Weapons for bows) must reach the monster's defence; a
  monster hits on d10 + its attack against 8 + Combat + half the armour. Weapons have damage
  ranges. The first three party members take most of the blows. Parry makes you harder to hit.
  Hide makes monsters ignore you and doubles your next hit. Clerics and paladins can turn undead.
  Bows need arrows somewhere on the character (they are picked up again after the fight).
- Spells: mages (Fire Dart), enchanters (Slumber) and clerics (Mend) with at least 1 in Magic
  get a mana pool of 2 + 3 × Magic (the original kept max magic at 0).
- Winning gives experience, silver and sometimes an item. Experience is scaled so grinding does
  not pay: a fight at a character's own power (best fighting ability + Toughness, + 1 for every 2 raises bought in other abilities) gives 1% of
  their next raise, each monster level above doubles it, each below halves it, and three below
  gives nothing. Hiding the whole fight gives nothing, falling halves it. Most experience comes
  from the story.
- **Wounds and death:** at 0 hits a character falls, badly wounded, and is out of the fight. Hits
  go on below 0; past 3 + Toughness below 0 they die. Monsters still strike the fallen in the
  front rank now and then. There is no raising the dead: a dead companion leaves the party and
  the others take their gear and silver. If the host dies, the game is over (load a save). After
  a deadly fight anyone still down comes around with 1 hit but **badly wounded**: their hits
  can't rise above half ("Wounded" by the portrait, on the sheet and at the inn) until they have
  rested at the inn, so a Cleric's Mend during the fight is worth a lot. If everyone who stayed
  falls, they wake at the last checkpoint, badly wounded and robbed of half their silver. The
  Warden knocks people down but never wounds badly or kills, and the prologue's road ambush
  neither.
- **Food on the road:** a day of travel costs 20 Endurance. Each evening everyone eats a
  ration from the packs (bread, dried fish, cheese), the best at Survival may forage, and the
  travel question shows how many days of food the packs hold. Out of Endurance a character is
  weakened (−1 on checks and to hit) and, on the road, starving (loses hits, never below 1). The
  inn's *Provisions* sells bread and dried fish. The first trip to the Thuug marshes has two
  scenes on the road (`scripts/data/marsh_road.gd`).
- **Oggal, the marsh village:** the Drowned Shrine on the world map leads into a Thuug stilt village,
  walked like Lehan's streets (`scripts/screens/village.gd`): Orrun at the causeway gate, Ubba
  and her story of the iron-crowned king, Grath-Ul the bored wrestler, Pim, Old Moss, Hett's
  stall (the shop screen for the marsh) and the guest hut (rest, and the village's checkpoint).
- **The shrine grounds:** the causeway east of Oggal leads into the Drowned Shrine's overgrown
  grounds, open to the sky, walked by the same dungeon screen as the mines (`Game.site`). Bog
  Leeches, Reed Stalkers, Marsh Lights (which lure someone away) and a Mire Toad; three sigils to
  find; a carving that tells their order (Lore); Old Moss's fish pens to clear (fight, or smoke
  with Survival) for Ubba's chant; and the sunken door, opened by the sigils, the chant, force or
  the lock (the last two loudly).
- **The drowned temple:** behind the sunken door, steps lead down into the shrine's temple: dark,
  dressed stone, the low halls flooded knee-deep. The Drowned (dead priests) and leeches; if the
  door was forced or picked, they wait at the foot of the steps. The sect's broken ward and fresh
  boot prints; a priest's niche; two carvings of the king's reign (Lore: his glory in the west,
  his fall in the east). The flooded hall is the test: rows of stepping stones, each carved with
  a sign, one right stone per row (crown, rose, fire, chain). A wrong one throws the party back
  across the hall. Both carvings, Ubba's skipping rhyme (asked in Oggal after seeing the hall),
  or the spirit's own memory (it hurts the host) tell the way. At the basin the remnant shows a
  memory of the king's reign and merges with the spirit.
- **R2 Insight:** after the merge, the character sheet shows everyone's nature (traits, nerve,
  a grudge), not only the host's, and the conversation window's portraits show the companions'
  traits. "Start at..." has "The drowned temple" for testing.
- **The north road to Aubermont:** after the merge the spirit feels the next remnant "north,
  behind iron", and Aubermont, the great city of the north, appears on the world map, four days
  from Lehan. The first trip has two scenes (`scripts/data/north_road.gd`): a toll bridge over
  the Aube whose guards ask who comes from Lehan (pay, talk, or ford the river; a Thuug swims a
  rope across), and Odo Brakk the gravedigger by the road (help, pray, or rob the dead).
  "Start at..." has "The north road".
- **Aubermont, the river gate district:** walked in first person (`scripts/screens/city.gd`,
  `scripts/areas/aubermont_streets.gd`, people in `scripts/data/aubermont.gd`). Tall soot-black
  houses whose upper storeys jut out further with every floor, so they lean over the narrow
  streets and nearly meet overhead; lit gothic windows, lanterns, wet cobbles, spires, a low grey
  sky. The market square has the Founders' statue (five lords round a broken crown; one crest,
  a tower and a key, has had its name chiselled off). Nan Grebe (a runaway servant, the grey
  "Quiet"), Sergeant Voss at the hill gate (Founders' Night and its guest lists), the crier, Edrin
  Calloway and his ancient tune (the spirit knows it), and Wick the boy guide. Corvin's Arms (city
  steel at city prices) and the Lamplighter, an inn like Lehan's (the same screen: muster,
  dismiss, rest, provisions, save; the city's checkpoint). Each inn keeps its own followers:
  those left at one inn wait there, out of reach from the other. Map (M) shows a plan of the
  district; click a place to walk there. The inn screen shows the inn's own room, with a fire
  burning in the hearth. The old town and the hill come next. "Start at..." has "Aubermont, the river gate".
- **Days, rest and checkpoints:** the game counts days (travel, each night's Sleep, a night at
  the inn). At the inn, *Dismiss* on a badly wounded companion leaves them to rest for 2 days at
  3 silver a day; mustering them sooner gets "Not yet. One more day.", and they heal while the
  party is away. *Rest a night* (1 silver a head) gives the party its hits and mana back (not
  past a bad wound) and lets a day pass. Each camp in the mines, and the inn, is a
  **checkpoint**: reaching it saves the game in the automatic slot, and it is where the party
  wakes after falling. Lines of companions who have died are left out of the scenes.
- **Daring to stay:** each round under pressure (own hits low, friends down, dead or fled, foes
  outnumbering or outclassing the party) every companion but the host checks d10 + Nerve against
  3 + pressure. A first failure and they waver (amber portrait, "Wavering"), a second and they
  flee (greyed out, back after the fight, no experience or loot). Nerve comes from the class,
  the traits (Brave, Cowardly, Loyal while a friend is down) and the raises bought. **Rally** (R)
  uses d10 + Talk to steady a wavering friend (+2 Nerve for the fight) or call back one who fled
  (they return the next round and do get experience). See `scripts/combat.gd`.

## Controls in the mines

W/↑ forward, S/↓ back, A/← and D/→ turn, Q/E step sideways, Space use or search, M map, J quests.
In combat: A Attack, P Parry, S Spells, U Use, E Equip, T Turn, R Rally, F Flee, H Hide, X Pass,
D Defaults, Esc back, Space hurry.

Everywhere: Esc closes what is open and goes back: a dialog (as Cancel or OK), the map, the
quest log, the character sheet (as Leave), the smith's shop.

## Developer aid

**Checkpoints** for testing by hand: *Start at...* on the title screen, or
`godot-4 --path . -- --start=market --host=tikkat`. The checkpoints are `road` (just after the
prologue), `lehan` (at the gate, before the employer), `split` (the inn after the split),
`market` (the market square, before the crier), `tunnels` (Skee's quest, in the old tunnels),
`council` (after the crier, at the council
house's door, the company still at the inn), `mines` (job taken, company back together, at the
entrance), `mines2` and `mines3` (the levels before played: their quest values, chests and gear,
and the company one or two steps stronger, a raise in each one's best fighting ability and in
Toughness per level, with half a raise of experience left to spend). The
host is any of joss, maera, ommuk, ithrae, tikkat. The story before the checkpoint is filled in
with simple choices (see `scripts/checkpoints.gd`).


**The tour:** `tools/tour.sh` visits every screen, saves screenshots into `.tour/`, and runs a few
rule checks (see `scripts/dev_tour.gd`). `tools/tour.sh quests` runs only the Lehan quests and the
council, and `tools/tour.sh mines` the mines job from the entrance to the report. The whole tour
takes under a minute: the script turns vsync off (so a hidden window isn't slowed to a frame a
second) and runs time 5x faster (`TOUR_TIME_SCALE` changes it). The log is `.tour/tour.log`, and
the tour writes its own saves (`tour_save_*.json`), never the player's. By hand it is
`godot-4 --path . -- --tour=$PWD/.tour [--part=quests|mines]`. It also simulates 200 auto-fights
at several depths and prints the win rate, which is handy when tuning monsters in `scripts/combat.gd`.
