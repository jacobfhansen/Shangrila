# Shangrila – ideas and next steps

A working list of what to do next. Each item says what it is, why, and any open questions.
Items marked **Open question** need a decision before work starts.

## Next steps

### Own monster names
The mines have Giant Rats, Cave Bats, Kobolds, Goblins, Giant Spiders, Skeletons, Orcs and
Renegades (`scripts/combat.gd`). These names come from folklore, so nobody owns them, but home-made
monsters would match the home-made races.
- The **Giant Rat** could be confused with the Nisk (rat-folk). Rename it, or make it an animal the
  Nisk keep or hunt.
- **Open question:** rename all of them, or only the ones closest to familiar fantasy (Kobold,
  Goblin, Orc)?

### Experience and levels
Winning fights gives experience, but it does nothing yet (the 1997 original had no levels).
- **Open question:** what does a new level give (hit points, skill points, mana, new spells)?

### Shop: Talk and Haggle
In the weapon smith, Talk and Haggle only print flavour text. Haggle could use the Haggle skill to
lower prices, and Talk could give rumours and hints about the mines.

### Examine in the mines
Examine only prints a message. It could find hidden things, read signs or spot traps (Perception
skill), which would also make the Nisk Perception bonus useful.

### Unfinished map
Six passages in the mines lead to squares the original never defined, so they show as rubble.
Extend the map past them, or keep them as rubble that could be cleared later.

### Art
The 47 portraits and the item icons were lost from the original's resource forks, so most are drawn
in code. Better home-made art could replace them, including portraits for the four races.

## Ideas

### World map and travel
**Started:** the map screen, travel along routes, and the Endurance cost per day are in the game
(Lehan and the Mines, one day apart). Still to do: discovering new places, running out of
food (weakened, then losing hit points), and the open questions below.

Bring a world map into play. The party travels between locations (cities, castles, dungeons such
as the mines of Lehan), and travel costs food, so moving around is never free.

**Map**
- A home-made world map generated by code (coasts, mountains, forests, rivers) in a parchment
  style, like the generated textures. It replaces `The_Map.png`, which is a scan of a real
  US map and must go.
- Only discovered locations, and the routes between them, are shown.

**Travel**
- Travel happens along known routes between locations. Pick a destination, and the trip takes a
  number of days.
- Each day of travel uses up Endurance (the apple bar) for every character. Endurance is already
  the food meter: food fills it, and Sleep spends it on healing.

**Running out of food**
- When a character's Endurance reaches 0, they are first *weakened* (lower stats and skills
  until they eat).
- If the starvation goes on, they start losing hit points.

**Discovering locations**
- **By travelling:** only visible places such as cities and castles can be found by travelling
  near them. Other places are plot-only and need one of the ways below.
- **Rumours:** Talk at the inn or a shop can mark a place on the map (ties in with the shop
  Talk item under Next steps).
- **Found maps and clues:** loot in dungeons can reveal places.
- **Mine exits:** passages in dungeons can lead out to new places (for example the rubble
  passages in the mines of Lehan).

**Open questions**
- How much Endurance does a day of travel cost, and how many days are typical trips?
- Can things happen on the road (monsters, travellers, weather), or is travel uneventful?
- Can food be bought (inn, market) or foraged on the way (the Foraging skill)?
- How long does it take to go from weakened to losing hit points, and how much do the
  stats drop?

### Characters with a will of their own
The player decides who is in the party, where it goes and what each character is ordered to do
in combat. But the characters are people: they like or dislike each other, and they do not
always follow orders.

**What shapes relations between characters**
- **Race attitudes:** fixed starting feelings between the races (for example, Thuugs distrust
  Nisk).
- **Personality traits:** each character has one or two traits (brave, greedy, pious,
  hot-tempered, loyal, …) that get on well or clash with other characters' traits. Traits are
  **hidden**. The player learns them only from the characters' comments during play ("Brand
  grumbles that the Nisk took the best share again").
- **Shared deeds:** feelings change with what happens. Saving someone in combat warms them, and
  unfair loot sharing or leaving a fallen friend behind sours them.
- **Values:** characters react to the party's choices. A paladin dislikes stealing or fleeing,
  and a thief scorns paying full price.

**Discipline (how well a character obeys)**
Built from:
- Will Power
- class training (warriors, paladins and monks are drilled; thieves and mages are more
  independent)
- experience (veterans keep their nerve, which finally gives experience a use)
- the party leader's Charisma, which lifts or lowers everyone's discipline

**In combat**
- **Small deviations:** a character with low discipline may swap an order for what they would
  rather do (a hot-head attacks instead of parrying, a coward hides). The log says why.
- **Morale breaks:** under heavy pressure (friends falling, losing badly), morale can break.
  The character flees, freezes or refuses to act.

**Outside combat, bad relations can lead to**
- **Refusing to join:** at the inn, a character will not muster into a party with someone
  they dislike.
- **Quarrels on the road:** arguments while travelling or resting cost Endurance or spoil sleep.
- **Leaving the party:** if it gets bad enough, a character walks out and returns to the inn.
- **Stealing or betrayal:** a greedy or hateful character might take loot or abandon someone.

**Open questions**
- How much should the player see? Only comments, or also a mood indicator (a face or colour
  per character) without numbers?
- Who is the party leader: the first slot, or chosen by the player?
- The list of traits, and the starting attitude table between the four races.
- Can the player improve relations (sharing loot fairly, feasting at the inn, a leader with
  high Charisma talking to them)?

### Race secrets
The races are new to the player, so they can still surprise. Each race has hidden traits that
come to light during play, a mix of gifts and drawbacks.

- **At the start:** the create screen keeps the short description and the stat bonuses. The
  secrets come on top of those.
- **How secrets are revealed:**
  - **Situations:** a trait shows the first time it matters (first river crossing, first
    night in the deep mines, first visit to a city shop).
  - **Character comments:** characters say things about their own kind.
  - **NPCs and books:** innkeepers, scholars and found books tell race lore.
  - **Journal:** every discovery is written into an in-game journal the player can reread.
- **Who writes them:** Claude proposes secrets, and Jacob picks, changes or drops them.

**Proposed secrets (to pick from)**

*Thuug*
- T1 **Cold-blooded:** sluggish in cold places (mountain passes, winter, deep mines), with lower
  Dexterity. Quicker in warm places.
- T2 **Sink like stones:** cannot swim, but can hold their breath for a long time and walk
  across river bottoms where others need a ford or a boat.
- T3 **Long fasts:** travel uses only half as much of their Endurance, but when they eat, they
  eat a lot.
- T4 **Moulting:** every few weeks the shell is shed. For a few days there is no armour bonus,
  and the Thuug is touchy.
- T5 **Old roads:** Thuugs live long and remember ancient routes. While travelling, a Thuug
  may reveal a forgotten route on the world map.

*Aeldroe*
- A1 **Light-fed:** sunlight restores their mana. In long darkness (deep mines) they fade: Hide
  improves, but they slowly lose Endurance.
- A2 **Colours show feelings:** when afraid or angry they flush with colour and become visible,
  so Hide fails. It also lets the player read their mood (ties into "will of their own").
- A3 **Iron-shy:** carrying iron weapons or armour dulls their magic (Magic Arts penalty).
- A4 **Dreamless:** sleep heals them less, but magic cannot put them to sleep.
- A5 **Ghost-sight:** they sense undead in nearby squares before the undead appear.

*Nisk*
- N1 **Whiskers:** no penalty in darkness. They may sense hidden passages, such as a way
  through the rubble in the mines.
- N2 **Hoarders:** they cannot resist shiny things. They find extra loot, but sometimes
  pocket coins for themselves (ties into stealing and betrayal).
- N3 **Iron stomach:** rotten food and poison do not harm them. They can eat what others
  cannot.
- N4 **Mistrusted:** city shopkeepers charge more and guards watch closely when a Nisk is in
  the party.
- N5 **Pack sense:** a Nisk fights better next to another Nisk, and gets nervous when alone
  (morale).

*Human*
- H1 **Quick learners:** they gain experience faster.
- H2 **Restless:** long stays in town make them bored and irritable (morale drops when idle).
- H3 **Welcome everywhere:** easier to get rumours from NPCs in any city.
- H4 **Ambitious:** they want to lead, and quarrel more if they are not the party leader.

### The player is a patron spirit
The player is not one of the characters. The player is a spirit, a remnant of a fallen ruler
(see "The spirit's forgotten past"), whose followers the characters are. The spirit cannot
act in the world by itself: it needs followers to cling to, and they are not puppets. They
believe in it, more or less. This frames the other ideas:
- **A small secret circle:** the spirit's followers are few, and they behave just like the
  jailers: a secret sect. There are no great hosts of believers and no temple building.
- **Orders** in combat are the spirit's nudges. Discipline is partly devotion ("Characters with
  a will of their own").
- **Hidden traits and race secrets:** the spirit cannot read minds. It learns about its
  followers and their races by watching and listening ("Race secrets", the journal).
- *Shangrila* was the realm the spirit once ruled (see "The spirit's forgotten past").

**Faith**
The spirit's power is measured in faith points.
- **Gained by:**
  - victories and deeds (won fights, quests, discovered places)
  - prayer: followers resting at inns or in hidden sanctums
  - answered prayers: when the spirit helps, believers grow more devoted
  - secret influence: followers quietly win influence in towns (informants, safe houses).
    It gives faith, but also risks discovery by the sect.
- **Spent on:**
  - **miracles:** small interventions such as a heal, a calmed quarrel, or a nudge to obey
  - **saving the fallen:** bringing a dead follower back
- **Lost when followers die:** every death costs the spirit faith, which must be won back.

**Death**
Death is permanent, unless the spirit spends faith to bring the follower back. (Today a
fallen character just comes around with 1 hit point, and a defeated party wakes up at the inn.
That changes.)

**Losing the host: game over**
If the host dies and no follower is close enough to take over, the game is over. The player
loads the last save or checkpoint. (This replaces the earlier idea of the spirit going
dormant.)

**The spirit's character sheet**
A separate sheet for the spirit itself (the player), apart from the followers' sheets. It
keeps track of the spirit's own stats: for example faith, level, remnants merged, powers and
miracles, the follower cap, and recovered memories.

**Follower cap**
How many characters the spirit can hold, in the party and waiting at inns together, is kept
small. It starts at 6 and grows a little, to around 10–12 at most, through remnants (R5).
(Today the limit is 23 at the inn plus 6 in the party.)

**Parking followers, and the danger of it**
- The player can park followers in a town to put together a party better suited to a
  specific task.
- **The sect strikes at parked followers.** When the party returns to a town to pick
  someone up, assassins may ambush them, and the party must fight to save them.
- **Warning signs** come first, such as strangers asking questions at the inn, so the
  player can hurry back or move them.
- **Safer places:** some places protect parked followers better, such as hidden sanctums
  (R7) or a loyal innkeeper.

**Open questions**
- Exactly how much does the cap grow, and with which remnants?
- Does the spirit have a name, or does the player choose one at the start?
- How does the spirit appear in the game: a voice in the log, an icon that shows faith?
- Which miracles exist, and what do they cost? Are they always available, or only when a
  follower prays?
- How expensive is bringing someone back, and is there a time limit (the body must be
  carried to a shrine)?

### The spirit's forgotten past (main story)
The spirit wakes with no memory of what it was. Uncovering its true origin and nature is the
backbone of the story, a mystery told in fragments.

**How memories come back**
- **Faith thresholds:** each new level of faith brings back a memory. The stronger the spirit
  grows, the more it remembers.
- **Dreams of followers:** when the party sleeps, a follower may dream a fragment of the past.
- **Ruins and old shrines:** forgotten places of the spirit's old worship, plot-only locations
  on the world map ("World map and travel").
- **People who remember:** old NPCs, hostile priests or other spirits who knew it before.
- Every fragment goes into the journal.

**Tone:** tragic. Whether the story ends in redemption or in more darkness is up to the player.

**The backstory** (what the player uncovers, piece by piece)
- **The ruler of Shangrila:** the spirit was once a powerful individual in a world where
  magic exists. It commanded godlike powers and ruled the realm of Shangrila with
  benevolence, defeating its enemies in glorious battles.
- **The one thing it could not command: love.** The spirit loved one particular woman, an
  Aeldroe, and she did not return its love. She loved someone else.
- **Jealousy:** the spirit had her lover slain or disgraced.
- **Despair:** the beloved killed herself.
- **Civil war:** her people, the Aeldroes, revolted. The spirit crushed the rebels with
  great cruelty. The Aeldroes' fading, their half-transparency, is the spirit's curse on
  them from that war. Only the Aeldroe elders know why their people fade: it is kept among
  them as "the secret". Ordinary Aeldroes do not know. (This becomes an Aeldroe race secret,
  and Aeldroe followers may react to the spirit in special ways.)
- **The fall:** to end the mad bloodshed, the once loyal nobles turned on the spirit,
  overthrew it and entombed it in the mines of Lehan. They swore an oath to guard the grave
  so that it could never return.
- **The remnants:** before its death the spirit managed to split off remnants of itself
  and hide them across the world, so that it could one day return and take revenge on its
  enemies. The player is one of them. Once the player learns the truth, they decide
  whether that revenge happens.
- **The centuries since:** plague and war ravaged the lands, Shangrila fell into ruin and
  legend, and most people forgot what it had all been about.
- **The sect:** a select few zealots, heirs of the nobles, still keep the oath. They are
  fanatic followers of what is now a secret sect, unknown to most (see "The jailers: hidden
  among us"). The sect knows about the remnants and hunts them too, to destroy them or keep
  them from the spirit.

**The player's choice: redemption or relapse**
The player can try to redeem the spirit, or simply continue where it left off, doing
terrible things to its enemies. The key choices along the way (see "Runs and endings")
decide which.
- **The ultimate redemption:** ending the Aeldroes' curse. This is the clearest proof that
  the spirit has changed, and the heart of the good ending.
  - **It has a price: the spirit's life.** Lifting the curse costs the spirit its life,
    and the game ends there: the spirit gives itself up to free the people it cursed.
  - **It can only happen after the body rises,** at the very end (see "The ending").
  - Ending the game at that moment also avoids having to show the Aeldroes changed
    afterwards (no longer transparent, and so on).
- **The direct path to the evil ending:** silencing the Aeldroe elders to protect the
  spirit's secret.

**How this fits the earlier origin proposals:** O1 (Shangrila, now the realm it ruled),
O2 (sealed in the mines, now by its own nobles), O3 (remnants, now hidden by the spirit
itself) and O5 (the Aeldroes, now the beloved's people, cursed) all live on in changed form.

**Open questions**
- Who was the beloved, and who was her lover? (Their identities are kept a mystery for now.)
- Was the lover slain or disgraced? Either way, the lover's line lives on: Edrin Calloway
  (see CHARACTERS.md) is a descendant.
- Where are the ruins of Shangrila, and can they be visited?
- How can the player learn "the secret" from the elders, and how do the elders react if
  they recognise what the spirit is? Do they tell their own people?
- What does the sect do with a remnant it finds: destroy it, lock it away, or use it?

**All proposed origins** (kept for reference; the backstory above replaces them)
- O1 **The lost guardian of Shangrila:** the spirit once watched over a hidden valley,
  Shangrila. Its people were scattered or destroyed, faith died out, and the spirit faded.
  Finding Shangrila again is the goal.
- O2 **The sealed one:** other powers bound the spirit for something it did, or was blamed
  for. The mines of Lehan were dug toward its prison, and the deeper the party goes, the more
  it remembers. Was the sealing just?
- O3 **The shattered spirit:** the spirit is one fragment of a greater being that was broken
  apart. Other fragments are rival spirits with followers of their own. Growing means
  winning them over, or absorbing them.
- O4 **The mortal who ascended:** the spirit was once a mortal adventurer (of which race?)
  who reached Shangrila and became something more. Its memories are of a mortal life, and
  its old companions may still have descendants.
- O5 **The faded people:** the Aeldroes are half-transparent because their ancestors were the
  spirit's first followers. When the faith ended, they began to fade with it. This could be
  combined with any of the others and become an Aeldroe race secret.
- O6 **The erased name:** a spirit's power lies in its name, and a rival temple wiped out every
  record of it. Pieces of the name are found one by one. Who erased it, and why?

### The spirit's prison and the journey
The spirit's own nobles overthrew it and buried its body in a sealed tomb deep in the mines
of Lehan. Before it died, the spirit split off remnants of itself and hid them across the world. The player is one
remnant: the one in the roadside shrine, woken after eons by the party camping there.
To be whole and free again, the spirit must find and merge with every other remnant, then
open the tomb and enter its body.

(Words used here: the *host* is the follower the spirit rides in; the *body* is the
spirit's own buried body in the tomb.)

**The host**
- The spirit rides inside one follower, the *host*. It can only see and act where the host is.
  The host is the spirit's anchor, not the player's avatar.
- Being the host matters: extra devotion and a channel for miracles, but also strain (for
  example nightmares, or the other followers' unease). This ties into "Characters with a will
  of their own".
- If the host dies, the spirit jumps to another follower nearby. If no follower is near, the
  game is over ("Losing the host: game over").

**From one party to many**
- **Early game:** the spirit travels only with its host's party. Followers left at inns in
  other towns are out of its reach.
- **Later (unlocked):** free travel. The spirit can jump between followers in different
  towns and manage several small groups.
- **Followers the spirit is not with live their own lives:** they build secret influence,
  earn and spend money, quarrel, and may even leave or die (or be attacked by the sect, see
  "Parking followers, and the danger of it"). When the spirit returns, it learns what
  happened.

**The prison in the mines**
- Deep in the mines lies the gate to the sealed tomb that holds the spirit's body. The party
  can reach the gate early, see that it cannot be opened yet, and must leave again. The
  player knows the final goal from the start.
- The unfinished rubble passages in the mines could become the way toward the chamber.
- Going deeper brings back memories ("The spirit's forgotten past").

**The remnants** (these replace the earlier idea of locks)
- There are **6–9 remnants**. The other remnants all sleep in hidden places, like the
  roadside shrine was. Finding them takes clues and items, and reaching them takes travel
  and strength.
- **Merging:** a found remnant merges with the travelling spirit at once. This levels the
  spirit up (shown on the spirit's character sheet).
- **Each remnant brings:**
  - **memories:** a piece of the spirit's past ("The spirit's forgotten past")
  - **a new power or mechanic, or a stronger version of one:** see the proposals below
- **Finding them, as a chain:** the memories in one remnant can point to where the next one
  sleeps. This keeps the guided story chosen earlier for the locks.
- They can be spread across the regions, and races, of the world. For example, Thuugs
  remember old roads, Nisk tunnels reach hidden places, and the Aeldroes carry the spirit's
  curse.
- **Remnant powers** (all nine are kept; which remnant brings which is decided later):
  - R1 **Free travel:** jump between followers in different towns ("From one party to many").
  - R2 **Insight:** see followers' hidden traits and moods directly, instead of only through
    comments.
  - R3 **Miracles:** the first miracles, and later stronger ones.
  - R4 **Dream-sending:** speak to followers, and potential followers, in their dreams.
    A new way to persuade and to reveal memories.
  - R5 **A wider reach:** a slightly higher follower cap, and followers further away can
    take over as host.
  - R6 **Taking a new host at will:** move into another follower in the party, not only when
    the host dies.
  - R7 **Hidden sanctums:** make secret hiding places (a cellar, a cave) where followers can
    rest safely, pray, and be parked out of the sect's reach.
  - R8 **Command:** a stronger hold on followers in combat (better discipline and morale).
  - R9 **Return from death:** bring a dead follower back ("Saving the fallen").

**The ending**
1. When every remnant is merged, the gate to the tomb can finally be opened or destroyed,
   after an epic boss battle.
2. The spirit enters its buried body, which rises from the dead.
3. **The sect's last stand:** a second epic boss battle. The sect makes its final attempt to
   stop the risen spirit, right before the final choice. It is led by the client who paid
   to have the roadside shrine destroyed at the start of the game.
4. **The final choice:** only now, whole and alive again, can the spirit lift the
   Aeldroes' curse, at the cost of its life (see "The player's choice: redemption or
   relapse"). Or it can keep its power and take its revenge. For maximum drama, the spirit
   must give up everything just when it has won it all back.

**The jailers: hidden among us**
- The heirs of the nobles who overthrew the spirit still live on in plain sight. Most
  have forgotten the oath. A select few zealots still keep it, as a secret sect unknown to
  most people. They may hide among the founding families of Lehan, a respected order, or a
  guild.
- They tie into "People who remember", and could become allies, enemies or a moral choice
  as the truth comes out.
- **Rivals in the race for the remnants:** the sect hunts the remnants too. A remnant
  could be found already guarded, taken, or destroyed by them.
- **The sect leader crosses the party's path often:** the client from the shrine job (see
  "The awakening") keeps turning up throughout the game, hunting remnants, in the towns,
  or questioning people about the night at the shrine. This builds up to the final battle.
- **Two secret circles:** the spirit's followers and the sect mirror each other. Both are
  small, hidden, and hunting the same remnants.

**Open questions**
- Exactly how many remnants (6–9), and in what order are they found?
- Which powers go with which remnant? (With fewer than nine remnants, some bring two.)
- Who exactly are the jailers' heirs, and what do they know?
- How do followers away from the spirit report back (letters at the inn, a summary when the
  spirit returns)?
- Who guards the gate to the tomb (first boss)?
- Where are the checkpoints, and how often?
- Which stats does the spirit's character sheet have?

### Recruiting and the touch of the spirit
How the spirit wins people over, given that it has no body and no voice.

**The spirit acts only through its followers**
The circle recruits on the spirit's behalf, like a real secret sect. The followers make the
approach, pay, do the tasks and talk. The spirit adds what no ordinary person can: miracles,
guidance, and later dreams.

| Want | How it is done in practice |
|---|---|
| Gold | The party pays from its purse; the host or a follower hands it over. |
| An item | The party finds, buys or steals the item and gives it to them. |
| A task | The party does the job: a quest, a fight, a debt paid. |
| Persuasion | A follower talks. That follower's Charisma, race, traits, and testimony matter. |
| Faith and power | The spirit works a miracle *through* the host, in front of the person. |

**How the spirit communicates**
1. **With the host:** an inner voice. At first only urges, feelings and flashes ("You feel
   a cold certainty that the stranger is lying"). It grows clearer with each remnant.
   In the game: the spirit's lines in the log, in italics.
2. **With the touched:** they feel the spirit's guidance themselves, more and more clearly
   the more they are touched.
3. **With outsiders:** no contact, except through miracles they witness and the testimony
   of the touched. Later, R4 Dream-sending lets the spirit reach them in dreams.

**Touched by the spirit**
- **Every intervention is a touch.** When the player works a miracle on a character, or
  takes direct control of their actions (for example their combat orders), that character
  is touched by the spirit.
- **It helps:** touched characters do better and survive more often.
- **It is a risk:** being touched can frighten them. Too much, too soon, and they may leave.
  How they take it depends on their hidden traits (a Pious character may welcome it; a
  Skeptic or Superstitious one may be scared).
- **It grows gradually** in everyone who travels with the host. Sooner or later, each one
  **embraces** it (becoming a believer, with more devotion and discipline) or **rejects**
  it (and may leave).
- **The touched spread it:** they talk about it among themselves and to outsiders. Their
  testimony is a form of persuasion: "Mivvi tells Boddo that he sometimes feels the spirit
  guiding his actions, and feels empowered by it. Boddo can know it too, if he just
  believes."
- **The host:** the spirit can take anyone in the party as host. Being touched makes a
  better, steadier host.

**Control in combat has a price**
- The host is always under the player's control, without a touch (once the spirit is
  strong enough, see "A game that starts by playing itself").
- Characters the player does not control fight on their own, by their traits and default
  tactics (a hot-head attacks, a coward hides, a healer heals).
- Taking control of a character for a round is a touch: better results, but a step closer
  to fear or to belief.
- This ties together "Characters with a will of their own", the touch, and the default
  tactics and auto-combat already in the game.

**The recruiting conversation**
- Recruiting happens in short conversations with **dialogue choices**. Which follower
  speaks, and what they say, matters. The character's hidden traits decide the outcome.
- **Wants and deal-breakers:** besides what a character wants, they have deal-breakers
  about the party. An Honest character will not join a party with a known thief, a Merciful
  one is put off by Ruthless followers, and a Thuug with a grudge refuses if there is a Nisk
  in the party. Who you bring along matters, which gives parking followers a purpose.
- **The shrine party** joins for certain. Everyone else is a challenge.
- **Higher tiers want more:** for example a task *and* persuasion, or an item *and* a show of
  power.
- **Learning what someone wants is part of the challenge:** through rumours, by watching
  them, and from how they react in conversation. R2 Insight later shows it directly.

**Failure**
- Usually it means "not yet": come back better prepared.
- Some wrong approaches offend a character for good, closing the door for this run.

**Open questions**
- In the opening, when does the spirit gain full control of the host: after the first
  fight, after reaching Lehan, or after the first remnant?
- How is the touch shown: a hidden value, comments only, or a visible sign on the
  character (a faint glow on the portrait)?
- How quickly does it grow, and what makes someone reject it?
- What happens to a character who rejects it: leaves quietly, or might tell others,
  including the sect?

### A game that starts by playing itself
A new kind of game experience: the spirit has just woken and is weak, so at first the player
has very little control. Control grows step by step, and that growth *is* the tutorial.

1. **Watching:** the party goes about its job on its own. It walks to the task location,
   moves around, and the characters talk among themselves. The player watches.
2. **The first choice:** in a conversation, the player can suddenly choose the host's
   reply. It is the first real interaction, and the first chance to guide the group.
3. **The first fight:** the characters fight as they would by their traits. The spirit
   cannot yet command the host, only help: give the host a bonus, or prevent a wound.
4. **Full control of the host:** later the spirit can take over the host's actions
   completely (the host is never a touch). Touching the others comes after that.
5. From there, each remnant adds a new power or mechanic (R1–R9), so the game keeps
   opening up.

### Pre-made followers instead of rolled characters
The spirit is discovering who it is by gathering and guiding followers, so the player no
longer rolls up characters. Every character is pre-made and named, with a story of their own.

**Where they are**
- Each city or point of interest has a fixed number of characters who can be won over
  to the cause.
- The higher a character's level and stats, the harder or more expensive they are to win.

**Winning them over**
Each character wants something different, a mix of:
- **a specific item** they want
- **gold:** some can simply be hired
- **faith and power:** some are impressed by what the spirit can do
- **a task:** a personal quest, a debt paid, revenge
- **persuasion:** the host's Charisma, the spirit's renown in that town, and shared values

**Each character has a story**
A backstory, fixed hidden traits (see "Characters with a will of their own"), a personal
quest, and possibly knowledge about the remnants or the jailers' heirs.

**Runs and endings**
- **Start of a run:** see "The awakening" below. The choice of first soul flavours the
  whole run.
- **Endings** (good, evil, and others) are decided by key choices, for example at each
  remnant. Different followers make different runs possible: who the spirit has gathered
  affects which choices are available and how they turn out.
- Playing again with other followers gives a different story and possibly a different ending.

**The awakening (opening of every run)**
- The spirit awakens at a roadside shrine on the road to Lehan, about a day from the town. It
  is the first location on the world map.
- A party of 4–5 adventurers, of mixed race and class, is camping at the shrine that night.
- The player chooses one of them, and the spirit takes control of that person **in secret**.
  The host is the spirit's first follower.
- **The group's job becomes the first quest:** the party was on its way to finish a job,
  and the spirit (the player) carries it through with them. It also teaches the game.
- **The party stays together for the job.** Afterwards, each companion decides whether to stay
  (and perhaps become a believer) or go their own way. Those who leave can be found again
  in the world and won over later.
- **The shrine party joins for certain:** all five are companions from the start, so the
  game gets going without a struggle.
- **They are touched gradually:** the host acts strangely (miracles, odd knowledge, changed
  habits), and the companions begin to feel the spirit themselves. Each reacts according to
  who they are (see "Recruiting and the touch of the spirit").

**Chosen opening: J3, then J2**
1. **Tear down the shrine (J3):** the shrine has lain hidden under undergrowth for ages. The
   client, the leader of the sect (the jailers' heirs), has only just discovered it,
   recognised what it is, and paid the group to destroy it. That night the party camping
   there wakes the remnant, and the spirit takes one of them as its host.
   The client returns at the very end as the second boss, leading the sect's last stand
   (see "The ending"). The first scene and the last are tied together.
   The spirit has no attachment to the shrine: it has just woken and its journey begins in
   its new host. The job can be done as planned, and the group is paid.
2. **Clear the mines (J2):** the group moves on to Lehan, where the council is hiring
   people to explore the old mine workings, where miners have vanished since something
   began to stir. That leads straight toward the spirit's prison, and teaches the dungeon.
3. After the mines job, each companion decides whether to stay or go their own way.

**All proposed first jobs** (J1, J4 and J5 were not chosen, kept for reference)
- J1 **The sealed box:** the group is escorting a sealed box to Lehan for one of its old
  families, which are secretly the jailers' heirs. The box holds something tied to one of the
  remnants, and the host can feel it. Deliver it, open it, or steal it?
- J2 **Clear the mines:** Lehan's council has hired them to explore the old mine workings,
  where miners have vanished since something began to stir (the spirit waking). It leads
  straight toward the spirit's prison and teaches the dungeon.
- J3 **Tear down the shrine:** someone paid the group to destroy the roadside shrine at
  dawn. The client is one of the jailers' heirs. The spirit wakes inside one of the people
  sent to destroy it.
- J4 **The renegade's bounty:** they are hunting a renegade (the surviving painting "The
  Enemy") who hides in the mines. The renegade turns out to be searching for the spirit too,
  for reasons of his own.
- J5 **The lost girl:** a miner's daughter, an Aeldroe, has gone missing in the mines. She
  followed a calling no one else could hear. A first hint of the Aeldroe secret.

**The roster:** the pre-made characters are listed in [CHARACTERS.md](CHARACTERS.md).

**What changes in the game today**
- The Create screen (rolling stats, picking race and class) goes away, or becomes a
  developer tool for making the pre-made characters.
- The inn roster becomes the followers the spirit has won. The pool of possible followers
  lives in the towns and places of the world map.
- This replaces the "follower cap" counting today's 23 + 6 limits (see "The player is a
  patron spirit").

**Open questions**
- How many characters per town?
- Who are the adventurers at the shrine, and why do they travel together?
- The client is the sect leader: what is their name and story? When do they first suspect
  that something escaped from the shrine?
- Can the player run out of followers? With permanent death and a fixed pool, the run could
  get stuck. Some characters could return later, or new ones appear as the story moves on.
- Do characters level up after joining (experience), or stay as they are?
- Is it right to read "different characters give different endings" as: followers decide
  which key choices are possible? Or should followers also shift the ending directly?
