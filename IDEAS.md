# Shangrila – ideas and next steps

A working list of what to do next. Each item says what it is, why, and any open questions.
Items marked **Open question** need a decision before work starts.

## Next steps

### Own monster names
The mines have Giant Rats, Cave Bats, Kobolds, Goblins, Giant Spiders, Skeletons, Orcs and
Renegades (`scripts/combat.gd`). These names come from folklore, so nobody owns them, but home-made
monsters would match the home-made races.
- The **Giant Rat** could be confused with the Nisk (rat-folk). Rename it, or make it an animal the
  Nisk keep or hunt.
- **Open question:** rename all of them, or only the ones closest to familiar fantasy (Kobold,
  Goblin, Orc)?

### Experience and levels
Winning fights gives experience, but it does nothing yet (the 1997 original had no levels).
- **Open question:** what does a new level give (hit points, skill points, mana, new spells)?

### Shop: Talk and Haggle
In the weapon smith, Talk and Haggle only print flavour text. Haggle could use the Haggle skill to
lower prices, and Talk could give rumours and hints about the mines.

### Examine in the mines
Examine only prints a message. It could find hidden things, read signs or spot traps (Perception
skill), which would also make the Nisk Perception bonus useful.

### Unfinished map
Six passages in the mines lead to squares the original never defined, so they show as rubble.
Extend the map past them, or keep them as rubble that could be cleared later.

### Art
The 47 portraits and the item icons were lost from the original's resource forks, so most are drawn
in code. Better home-made art could replace them, including portraits for the four races.

## Ideas

### World map and travel
**Started:** the map screen, travel along routes, and the Endurance cost per day are in the game
(Lehan and the Mines, one day apart). Still to do: discovering new places, running out of
food (weakened, then losing hit points), and the open questions below.

Bring a world map into play. The party travels between locations (cities, castles, dungeons such
as the mines of Lehan), and travel costs food, so moving around is never free.

**Map**
- A home-made world map generated by code (coasts, mountains, forests, rivers) in a parchment
  style, like the generated textures. It replaces `The_Map.png`, which is a scan of a real
  US map and must go.
- Only discovered locations, and the routes between them, are shown.

**Travel**
- Travel happens along known routes between locations. Pick a destination, and the trip takes a
  number of days.
- Each day of travel uses up Endurance (the apple bar) for every character. Endurance is already
  the food meter: food fills it, and Sleep spends it on healing.

**Running out of food**
- When a character's Endurance reaches 0, they are first *weakened* (lower stats and skills
  until they eat).
- If the starvation goes on, they start losing hit points.

**Discovering locations**
- **By travelling:** only visible places such as cities and castles can be found by travelling
  near them. Other places are plot-only and need one of the ways below.
- **Rumours:** Talk at the inn or a shop can mark a place on the map (ties in with the shop
  Talk item under Next steps).
- **Found maps and clues:** loot in dungeons can reveal places.
- **Mine exits:** passages in dungeons can lead out to new places (for example the rubble
  passages in the mines of Lehan).

**Open questions**
- How much Endurance does a day of travel cost, and how many days are typical trips?
- Can things happen on the road (monsters, travellers, weather), or is travel uneventful?
- Can food be bought (inn, market) or foraged on the way (the Foraging skill)?
- How long does it take to go from weakened to losing hit points, and how much do the
  stats drop?

### Characters with a will of their own
The player decides who is in the party, where it goes and what each character is ordered to do
in combat. But the characters are people: they like or dislike each other, and they do not
always follow orders.

**What shapes relations between characters**
- **Race attitudes:** fixed starting feelings between the races (for example, Thuugs distrust
  Nisk).
- **Personality traits:** each character has one or two traits (brave, greedy, pious,
  hot-tempered, loyal, …) that get on well or clash with other characters' traits. Traits are
  **hidden**. The player learns them only from the characters' comments during play ("Brand
  grumbles that the Nisk took the best share again").
- **Shared deeds:** feelings change with what happens. Saving someone in combat warms them, and
  unfair loot sharing or leaving a fallen friend behind sours them.
- **Values:** characters react to the party's choices. A paladin dislikes stealing or fleeing,
  and a thief scorns paying full price.

**Discipline (how well a character obeys)**
Built from:
- Will Power
- class training (warriors, paladins and monks are drilled; thieves and mages are more
  independent)
- experience (veterans keep their nerve, which finally gives experience a use)
- the party leader's Charisma, which lifts or lowers everyone's discipline

**In combat**
- **Small deviations:** a character with low discipline may swap an order for what they would
  rather do (a hot-head attacks instead of parrying, a coward hides). The log says why.
- **Morale breaks:** under heavy pressure (friends falling, losing badly), morale can break.
  The character flees, freezes or refuses to act.

**Outside combat, bad relations can lead to**
- **Refusing to join:** at the inn, a character will not muster into a party with someone
  they dislike.
- **Quarrels on the road:** arguments while travelling or resting cost Endurance or spoil sleep.
- **Leaving the party:** if it gets bad enough, a character walks out and returns to the inn.
- **Stealing or betrayal:** a greedy or hateful character might take loot or abandon someone.

**Open questions**
- How much should the player see? Only comments, or also a mood indicator (a face or colour
  per character) without numbers?
- Who is the party leader: the first slot, or chosen by the player?
- The list of traits, and the starting attitude table between the four races.
- Can the player improve relations (sharing loot fairly, feasting at the inn, a leader with
  high Charisma talking to them)?

### Race secrets
The races are new to the player, so they can still surprise. Each race has hidden traits that
come to light during play, a mix of gifts and drawbacks.

- **At the start:** the create screen keeps the short description and the stat bonuses. The
  secrets come on top of those.
- **How secrets are revealed:**
  - **Situations:** a trait shows the first time it matters (first river crossing, first
    night in the deep mines, first visit to a city shop).
  - **Character comments:** characters say things about their own kind.
  - **NPCs and books:** innkeepers, scholars and found books tell race lore.
  - **Journal:** every discovery is written into an in-game journal the player can reread.
- **Who writes them:** Claude proposes secrets, and Jacob picks, changes or drops them.

**Proposed secrets (to pick from)**

*Thuug*
- T1 **Cold-blooded:** sluggish in cold places (mountain passes, winter, deep mines), with lower
  Dexterity. Quicker in warm places.
- T2 **Sink like stones:** cannot swim, but can hold their breath for a long time and walk
  across river bottoms where others need a ford or a boat.
- T3 **Long fasts:** travel uses only half as much of their Endurance, but when they eat, they
  eat a lot.
- T4 **Moulting:** every few weeks the shell is shed. For a few days there is no armour bonus,
  and the Thuug is touchy.
- T5 **Old roads:** Thuugs live long and remember ancient routes. While travelling, a Thuug
  may reveal a forgotten route on the world map.

*Aeldroe*
- A1 **Light-fed:** sunlight restores their mana. In long darkness (deep mines) they fade: Hide
  improves, but they slowly lose Endurance.
- A2 **Colours show feelings:** when afraid or angry they flush with colour and become visible,
  so Hide fails. It also lets the player read their mood (ties into "will of their own").
- A3 **Iron-shy:** carrying iron weapons or armour dulls their magic (Magic Arts penalty).
- A4 **Dreamless:** sleep heals them less, but magic cannot put them to sleep.
- A5 **Ghost-sight:** they sense undead in nearby squares before the undead appear.

*Nisk*
- N1 **Whiskers:** no penalty in darkness. They may sense hidden passages, such as a way
  through the rubble in the mines.
- N2 **Hoarders:** they cannot resist shiny things. They find extra loot, but sometimes
  pocket coins for themselves (ties into stealing and betrayal).
- N3 **Iron stomach:** rotten food and poison do not harm them. They can eat what others
  cannot.
- N4 **Mistrusted:** city shopkeepers charge more and guards watch closely when a Nisk is in
  the party.
- N5 **Pack sense:** a Nisk fights better next to another Nisk, and gets nervous when alone
  (morale).

*Human*
- H1 **Quick learners:** they gain experience faster.
- H2 **Restless:** long stays in town make them bored and irritable (morale drops when idle).
- H3 **Welcome everywhere:** easier to get rumours from NPCs in any city.
- H4 **Ambitious:** they want to lead, and quarrel more if they are not the party leader.

### The player is a patron spirit
The player is not one of the characters. The player is a minor spirit (a forgotten power)
whose followers the characters are. The spirit cannot act in the world by itself: it needs
followers to cling to, and they are not puppets. They believe in it, more or less. This frames
the other ideas:
- **Orders** in combat are the spirit's nudges. Discipline is partly devotion ("Characters with
  a will of their own").
- **Hidden traits and race secrets:** the spirit cannot read minds. It learns about its
  followers and their races by watching and listening ("Race secrets", the journal).
- *Shangrila* could be what the spirit seeks, what it lost, or where it came from.

**Faith**
The spirit's power is measured in faith points.
- **Gained by:**
  - victories and deeds (won fights, quests, discovered places)
  - prayer: followers resting at inns or visiting shrines
  - answered prayers: when the spirit helps, believers grow more devoted
  - spreading the word: followers with high Charisma convert NPCs, so the faith grows in towns
- **Spent on:**
  - **miracles:** small interventions such as a heal, a calmed quarrel, or a nudge to obey
  - **saving the fallen:** bringing a dead follower back
- **Lost when followers die:** every death costs the spirit faith, which must be won back.

**Death**
Death is permanent, unless the spirit spends faith to bring the follower back. (Today a
fallen character just comes around with 1 hit point, and a defeated party wakes up at the inn.
That changes.)

**Losing every follower**
The spirit goes dormant until a new soul finds its shrine. The game starts over with a new
follower, but the spirit keeps some of what it learned (the journal, race secrets).

**Follower cap**
How many characters the spirit can hold, in the party and waiting at inns together, starts at
6 and grows as the faith grows. (Today the limit is 23 at the inn plus 6 in the party.)

**Open questions**
- What makes the cap grow, given that faith is not spent on it: total faith earned,
  the number of believers in towns, or milestones in the story?
- Does the spirit have a name, or does the player choose one at the start?
- How does the spirit appear in the game: a shrine at the inn, a voice in the log, an icon
  that shows faith?
- Which miracles exist, and what do they cost? Are they always available, or only when a
  follower prays?
- How expensive is bringing someone back, and is there a time limit (the body must be
  carried to a shrine)?
